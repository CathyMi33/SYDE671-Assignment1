A portrait from two distances

I took one photo close to the person with a wide view. Then I moved back and zoomed in. I cropped the display images so the face stays in the same place and is about the same size. Part 2

Images of the Russian Empire

Rebuilding colour photographs from three black-and-white glass-plate exposures.

01 / Method

From one plate to one colour photograph

Each old plate has three black-and-white photos stacked vertically. The top is blue (B), the middle is green (G), and the bottom is red (R). I keep B in place, move G and R until the details line up, and combine them into one colour photo.

Finding a match

I try different left-right and up-down shifts. I compare the middle of the photos to avoid damaged edges. L2 picks the shift with the smallest pixel difference. NCC picks the shift with the strongest match. The tables show how far G and R moved from B.

Image pyramid

Large TIFF files need a wider search. I first shrink each channel and find a rough shift. I then work back toward the full-size photo and improve the shift at each size. This makes the search faster.

Saving the result

I combine the aligned channels and remove pixels that do not overlap. I also save a second copy with its outer border cropped automatically. The original L2 and NCC results remain in separate folders.

02 / Small image

Single-scale alignment

I tested an exhaustive search over ±15 pixels on the low-resolution JPEG 31421v.jpg. The L2 and NCC searches both produced G = (0, 8) and R = (0, 13) relative to B. On this image, the pyramid returned the same offsets.

A second downsampled JPEG of the self-selected 00210u plate was also tested: both metrics found G = (-2, -2) and R = (-7, -1).

05 / Bells & Whistles

Detecting and trimming frame borders

The old plate frame can leave dark, white, or colourful strips around the photo. My code looks for long, strong edges near the outside. It crops outside a detected edge. If it cannot find a clear edge, it leaves that side alone.
